# Clawd Saver: The Dojo

Written 2026-08-12, the same day as
[the three-scene note](2026-08-12-three-more-scenes.md). The scene contract is
stated in [the four-scene note](2026-08-11-four-scenes.md) and widened once in
the three-scene one.

An eighth scene: fruit thrown up into the air and cut in half. The idea is
borrowed from a game everyone has played; the name is not.

---

## 1. Things flying in from off screen, on a stage that forbids it

The whole appeal is fruit arriving from somewhere else, and `checks/verify-scenes.js`
fails any rect that leaves the stage unless it is a backdrop or a particle. The
lesson from the parcel conveyor two scenes earlier still stood: a thing that
appears or disappears in open view reads as a glitch, however well motivated.

The answer is that a thrown object does not have to come from the side. It can
come from **below**, and below is somewhere the stage can hide.

```js
rect(-4, BASE, W + 8, H - BASE + 4, MAT_L);
rect(-4, BASE + 1, W + 8, H - BASE + 3, MAT);
```

A floor. It spans the full width and reaches the bottom, so it satisfies the
backdrop rule the jetty's sea already relies on, and it gives the fruit eight
units of stage to be launched from and land back into, unseen. Fruit Ninja
throws from the bottom of the screen too, so this is also just the reference
being right.

The floor is drawn deliberately close to the background colour. Its job is to
occlude, not to put a slab across the bottom of the picture.

## 2. Ballistics with no state

Three fruit share one cycle:

```js
const CYCLE = 2100, N = 3, BEAT = CYCLE / N;      // one at the top every 700 ms
const phase = (now, i) => (now / CYCLE + i / N) % 1;
const arcX = (from, span, p) => snap(from + span * p);
const arcY = (rise, p) => snap(BASE - rise * 4 * p * (1 - p));
```

At `p = 0` and `p = 1` the parabola puts a fruit exactly on the floor line, which
is to say under it. The phase wraps there, invisibly. Nothing is stored, nothing
resets, and a frame drawn at any instant is a real one.

Which fruit is which is a function of the flight rather than a stored field:
`Math.floor(now / CYCLE + i / N)` counts flights and does not change during one,
so the same expression gives both the phase and the identity.

## 2a. Both sides, which moves Clawd

They come in from both edges, so Clawd stands in the middle. That is the one
thing about him no other scene changes, and it needed `drawClawd` to take an `x`
offset: 15 units, which puts his centre on the stage's.

The two arcs are mirrored about him and launch near an edge, arcing inwards.
Neither reaches him, and that is a requirement rather than a coincidence: the
fruit are drawn behind him, so an arc that crossed him would take the fruit out
of sight for half its flight. What hides a fruit until it clears the floor is the
mat, which is drawn after them and before him.

Which side a fruit comes in on, and which side the blade goes to, are the same
number reached two ways. A flight tops out on the beat `3 * flight + 1 - i`, so a
fruit's side is that beat's parity, and the swing takes the parity of the beat it
is in. They cannot disagree, for the same reason the parcel line's stamp cannot
miss: there is one clock.

**He carries a sword in each hand.** One sword changing hands on the beat is a
sword that teleports every 700 ms, and no pose hides that. Only one of them
swings; the other holds a guard.

The swing is half a beat out of step with the beat itself, which is not a fudge:
a fruit reaches the top of its arc in the middle of its beat, not at the start of
one. Getting that wrong is what the first version did, and it swung at nothing
for a while before anyone noticed.

## 3. The cut is a phase, not an event

A fruit is at the top of its arc at `p = 0.5`, and that is where the blade meets
it. So `p > 0.5` **is** the state of being cut:

```js
p <= 0.5 ? null : Math.round((p - 0.5) * 20)
```

Past the apex the fruit is drawn as two halves, each moved by that number in
opposite directions, so the gap between them opens at twice the rate. Each
carries a strip of flesh colour on its cut face. They keep following the same
parabola and fall back under the floor. No flag, no timer, no list of live
objects. The whole life of a fruit is one number.

The one thing that genuinely cannot be derived is the *instant* of the cut, which
is an edge rather than a position, and juice has to be thrown at it. That is the
scene's only `step`, and it is the same shape as the mine's and the forge's.

## 4. Two things the size limits forced

**The blade points, it does not reach.** Aiming the tip at a fixed spot per pose
makes the sword change length as the arm moves, by about 60% between the raised
pose and the follow-through. Each pose carries a direction instead.

A direction is not enough on its own, though, which the first version got wrong:
the vectors were used raw, and `(3, 1)` is 41% longer than `(1, -2)`, so the
blade still grew and shrank once a beat with a bright block on the end marking
exactly how much. They are normalised now, and the length is one constant.

That also fixed a hole. `blockLine` lays its blocks at fractions of the line, so
the first one sat further from the hand the longer the pose happened to be: at
the follow-through it started four units out while the grip only reached two,
and background showed between them for a fifth of every beat.

**The fruit is clipped at the floor rather than drawn through it.**

```js
const bit = (x, y, w, h, c) => {
  const cut = Math.min(h, BASE - y);
  if (cut > 0) rect(x, y, w, cut, c);
};
```

Ten by eight is the smallest a fruit can be and still read as a thing being cut
in half rather than as a coloured pip. But a fruit that size, drawn whole while
it waits under the floor, hangs past the bottom of the stage and fails the bounds
check. Clipping at the floor line changes nothing anyone can see, because the mat
covers that part regardless, and it keeps every rect where the checker wants it.

## 5. The name

Not Fruit Ninja. The scene is called **The dojo**, keyed `dojo`, which says what
it is without borrowing the title. Clawd wears a headband for it, red with a pale
disc, with short tails trailing behind him: no crown, which separates it from the
printer's visor, and the tails run the opposite way from the parcel line's cap
peak so the two asymmetric hats are not the same asymmetry.

The accent is melon pink, `#FF6FA5`. The seven already taken sit at hues 5, 43,
50, 134, 175, 198 and 267 degrees; 338 was the one real gap left.

## What it cost elsewhere

**The dialog is up to fourteen rows**, in a fixed 440 by 700 window that holds
about ten of them. The stored scene is brought into view on open, and doing that
turned out to be harder than it reads.

The version added with the previous three scenes did not work. It scrolled the
scene row into view and then focused the selected period row, and `focus()`
scrolls to its own element: the second line silently undid the first, and for the
default period the last three scenes stayed below the fold. The smoke test could
not see it either, because its stub counts calls and has no layout.

So the scene row is what takes focus, and that is what scrolls it. It also has to
be that way round for a second reason: with fourteen rows in a ten-row window,
focus on a period row while the scene group is showing means the arrow keys are
driving a list that is off the top of the form.

**The headband took two goes.** The first was white and full brim width, which
made it the brightest thing on the stage after the counter and read as a plank
resting on his head rather than a band round it. Flush with the body and in a
deep red fixed both at once.

**The celebration fruit had to go in front of Clawd.** Drawn behind him, as the
other three are, its arc across the top spent half its flight inside his head.
Raising it is not an option: clearing the headband needs an apex that puts the
fruit's top at y 24, which is the amount row. It is the one thing in the scene
drawn after him.
