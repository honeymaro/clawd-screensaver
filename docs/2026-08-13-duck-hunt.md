# Clawd Saver: Duck Hunt

Written 2026-08-13. The scene contract is stated in
[the four-scene note](2026-08-11-four-scenes.md) and widened twice since.

A ninth scene, and the first that is somebody else's game rather than a job. It
keeps the title, because "The retriever" would have been coy about a nod that is
obvious anyway.

---

## 1. What the game actually is, and what survives the move

Duck Hunt shipped on the Famicom in 1984 and came in the box with the NES in
North America the year after, played with the Zapper light gun. There are three
modes: one duck, two ducks, and clay pigeons. You get three shells a round, and
ducks that outlast them fly off with "FLY AWAY!" across the screen.

What makes a picture of it recognisable, and what happened to each part here:

| | |
|---|---|
| **The dog.** Sniffs through the grass, flushes the ducks, holds up what you hit, laughs at what you miss | **Clawd is the dog.** He was always going to be; that was the first thing decided |
| **A hit duck freezes in the air and drops straight down** | Kept, and it is the best thing in the scene to implement |
| Grass in the foreground, distant cover behind it | Kept |
| A bright blue sky | **Dropped**, see below |
| The bottom bar: round, hit markers, three shells | Dropped. There is already a counter on this stage and it is at the top |
| The laughing dog | **Not in this version**, see below |

**The sky.** This program picked a dark palette on purpose, and wrote down why:
a full-screen light background is punishing at night and costs more on an OLED
panel. A bright blue field is the single loudest thing the stage could contain
and it would break that for one scene. So there is no sky, only what stands in
front of one: turf, and a line of cover behind it, on the same near-black the
other eight use.

**The laugh.** It is the most famous two seconds in the game and it is not in
here. A screensaver that mocks you every fifteen seconds is a different product,
and "the duck got away" does not correspond to anything the counter can do. Every
beat in this scene is one that ends well.

## 2. Nobody is holding a gun, but you can see where it is pointed

Deciding that Clawd is the dog decides the rest: there is no hunter on screen,
because there is no hunter on screen in Duck Hunt either. The player is off to
one side of a cathode ray tube with a plastic pistol. The stage shows a duck and
a dog, exactly as it did.

The shot itself is legible without a shooter, because the game already solved
that: the duck goes white for a frame, stops dead with its wing still up, and
falls like a brick.

What the game did not have is a sight. The Zapper read light off the glass, so
there was nothing to draw and nothing on screen between shots. This scene has
one anyway, and it is the one deliberate departure from the original. Without
it the picture is a duck that dies for no reason every 3.6 seconds; with it,
there is somebody out there.

**It must not follow the duck.** The first version had it trailing the bird by a
fraction of the climb and closing the gap, which sounds like tracking and looks
like a turret: the sight was on the target from the instant the target existed,
so the shot was always available and nothing about the climb built towards it.

What it does instead is what a person does. It sits where it was left, in the
middle of the empty half, and it stays there through the flush, because you do
not have the duck until you have seen it. Then it goes and gets it: fast out of
the gate, slow into the target, on it for a beat, fire. Afterwards it swings
back to where it waits, and for the two frames between the shot and the swing
it is not drawn at all, which is the flinch.

Every position it takes is a blend between where it rests and where the bird is,
on an ease that is fast then slow. That is the whole trick, and it means the
sight stores nothing between frames: at any instant it is a function of the
clock, like everything else here.

A ring seven units across with a bright pip at the centre of it. Half the duck,
and it sits on the bird rather than around it. That is where a crosshair goes:
it marks a point, it does not box a target. Two earlier versions got this wrong
in the same direction, corner brackets twenty two across and then a ring at
eleven, and both were a targeting computer acquiring a contact, which is a
different film.

The pip is not decoration. A ring on its own says roughly here; the single
bright unit in the middle of it says exactly there, and the difference between
those two is the difference between a marker and a sight.

A circle on a grid this coarse has to be spelled out, so it is: eight runs,
written down as offsets rather than worked out with arithmetic at each corner,
because the table is shorter than the maths and rounder than what the maths
would give.

It stays the same grey through the shot. Going white with the bird would lose
the ring inside the flash at the one instant it is the whole point.

The sight is drawn over Clawd as well as over the duck. It is on the glass, not
in the field.

## 3. One flight, and the whole beat inside it

3.6 seconds, and every part of it is a function of `now`:

| phase | |
|---|---|
| 0.06 to 0.52 | the duck climbs out of the turf, wings on a 120 ms cycle, wavering two units |
| 0.52 | hit: white for a frame, feathers |
| to 0.58 | stopped in the air |
| 0.58 to 0.74 | straight down, accelerating, into the grass |
| 0.76 to 1.00 | Clawd rises out of the grass with it, then sinks back |

At the ends of the climb and the fall the duck is under the turf line, so it is
never visible at the moment its phase wraps. That is the same trick the dojo's
fruit uses under its floor, and the jetty's sea before that: the only way found
so far to loop a thrown thing without a pop.

The only stored state is the moment of the last celebration. Everything else,
including whether the duck is currently alive, is arithmetic on the phase.

## 4. Composition, which took two goes

The first attempt put Clawd in the middle at his usual size with the turf high
enough to reach his chest. It did not work: he is 36 units wide on a 112 unit
stage, the grass and the cover behind it took another 26, and what was left for
the sky was a strip the duck could not cross. The subject of this scene is the
empty part of it.

Second attempt, and the numbers that fixed it:

- the turf drops to y 60 and the cover to y 52, which leaves 28 units of sky
- Clawd moves 26 units right, so the duck has the whole left of the stage
- he stands chest deep, and at rest only his cap, his ears and his eyes clear
  the grass. He comes up ten units to hand the duck over
- the duck grows to thirteen by fourteen, wing tip to wing tip

Ten units of lift is all there is: the cap's crown sits at y 29, the counter
reaches y 24, and the two have to stay apart. What carries the moment instead is
that the duck appears in his hands, held in front of his face, which reads as
carrying it in his mouth and is not a coincidence.

The duck is drawn in front of him. Behind him it vanished into his chest halfway
up the climb; in front it simply passes close.

None of these numbers had to move for the sight, which is the argument for
keeping it small. The bracket version needed the ceiling five units lower and
the shot twelve units left just to have somewhere to put its corners; a sight
that fits inside the bird leaves the composition alone.

## 5. The cap

A hunting cap with the flaps down, which is as near a dog's ears as Clawd gets
without stopping being Clawd. The flaps hang past his head on both sides and well
below it, and they are what makes the silhouette work: the first version had a
brim all the way round instead, which was a plank laid across him.

The crown is deliberately low, for the ten units above.

## 6. Accent

Grass green, `#A8D84A`, hue 80. The eight already taken sit at 5, 43, 50, 134,
175, 198, 267 and 338 degrees, and the run from 50 to 134 was the widest gap
left. It is on the stage as well as in the counter: a few blades standing proud
of the turf are the only saturated thing in the picture.

## What it cost elsewhere

**Fifteen rows in the dialog**, still in a fixed 440 by 700 window. The stored
scene is focused on open, which is what scrolls it into view; that mechanism was
fixed in the previous commit and is now doing real work, because a ninth scene
is nine rows below the fold.

**A second scene now clips at its ground line.** The dojo's fruit and this
duck are both drawn through a helper that trims anything below the turf, for the
same reason: the sprite has to be big enough to read, and one waiting under the
ground would otherwise hang off the bottom of the stage and fail the bounds
check. It is starting to look like something the shared layer should own rather
than each scene, if a third one needs it.
